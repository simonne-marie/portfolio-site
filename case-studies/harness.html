Case study · Harness

Cold calls, manual emails, and tools that never talked to each other

How I built Harness's first marketing engine as its first marketing hire

Role
Marketing Director
Company
Harness, recurring giving for nonprofits
Tenure
October 2021 to June 2023
Team
First marketing hire
HubSpot Salesforce Outreach

The situation

I was Harness's first marketing hire. Sales SDRs made cold calls, the team used Outreach to email contact lists, and customer success handled customer marketing on its own. Every team had the best tools for its job, but the company was paying for too many of them, features overlapped, and none of them talked to each other. Sales was in the middle of onboarding Salesforce.

The challenge

The CEO asked for two things. Four gaps sat behind them.

First, cut the cost of the tool stack. Second, build an inbound strategy that could reduce the need for SDRs over time. Behind those asks:

  • Too many tools, none connectedOverlapping features, and no data moving between systems.
  • Sales ran on cold outreachSDRs called and emailed lists, with no inbound engine. The hope was that customers would eventually come asking for Harness.
  • A leaky bucket after the saleNew customers met with customer success, but there was no product-led system. Customers who skipped the three initial setup tasks tended to fizzle out and drift away without seeing the product's value.
  • Manual customer emailsCustomer success sent every email by hand.

What I did

Two decisions first, then four moves

Answers: Too many tools, none connected

  • Audit the stack with every teamI met with every team leader, learned how they used their tools and where those tools hurt, and presented the overlaps. Together we narrowed the stack.
  • Bring in HubSpot and connect it to SalesforceI pitched HubSpot for marketing, the platform I know best, and worked with sales to manage the Salesforce integration while they onboarded.

Before

Overlapping features. Nothing connected.

After

One connected stack around HubSpot and Salesforce.

Answers: Sales ran on cold outreach

An inbound engine built on content

The content strategy had three jobs: improve SEO, build thought leadership in the nonprofit space, and give nonprofits a solution to their biggest problem, raising enough each year. Gated eBooks matched different buying stages, from why recurring giving matters to a buyer's guide comparing donation software.

Social and paid creative that pointed back to the content

Answers: Sales ran on cold outreach

A webinar series nonprofits wanted to attend

Nonprofits love absorbing information, so I launched and hosted a series that taught fundraisers to raise more efficiently and showed how subscription giving grows annual donations over time. I booked the guest experts, handled promotion and hosting, and turned every recording into evergreen content.

Answers: Sales ran on cold outreach

Warm leads handed to sales at the right moment

Content warmed leads up. Once a lead turned warm or hot, based on activity or lead score, sales reached out. Anyone who filled out the demo form was assigned to a rep automatically. For existing accounts, intent data and behavioral signals ranked who was most likely to buy or expand, using rules set with sales leadership.

Answers: A leaky bucket after the sale, and manual customer emails

A system for the customer after the sale

I built an automated onboarding flow to guide new customers through their first three tasks, replacing emails sent by hand. Email stats showed who never opened or responded, so the team could step in. For new features, I started from user research and the support queue, then wrote launch emails, social posts, and webinars that answered the questions customers were already asking.

The outcome

What changed

  • +11%customer operations satisfaction rating after launch messaging built from customer questions
  • 8webinars in the series I launched and hosted
  • 3gated eBooks, plus five more guides and resources
  • One connected stack: HubSpot for marketing and Salesforce for sales, with fewer overlapping tools.
  • Warm and hot leads routed to sales by activity or score, with demo requests assigned to a rep automatically.
  • Automated onboarding, with email stats that showed who needed a nudge.

One connected stack now served marketing, sales, and customer success.
