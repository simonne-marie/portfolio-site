The situation

When I joined HealthSnap, the marketing team was two people. HubSpot and ZoomInfo were brand new and still out of the box, and the company was moving its CRM from Zoho to Salesforce. All sales came from conferences and word of mouth. There was no inbound marketing engine.

The challenge

The business wanted inbound demand. Four gaps stood in the way.

  • Two systems, no shared lifecycleHubSpot and Salesforce needed common stages so sales could see each lead and its marketing history.
  • No shared definition of a sales-ready leadMarketing and sales had no agreed criteria for when a lead is ready for a sales conversation.
  • No link between activity and revenueChannels, content, and events could not be tied to booked meetings or closed revenue.
  • A leaky demo journeyThe demo button sent visitors to an outside scheduling page, which broke the experience and the tracking.

What I did

Two decisions first, then four moves

  • Audit the tools and the onboardingI reviewed the new tools and how they were being set up, removed redundancies, and mapped one user flow from lead to sales handoff. Then I launched it.
  • Align with sales on lifecycle stagesI agreed the stages with sales first, so the integration, the score, and the reports all started from the same definitions.

Answers: Two systems, no shared lifecycle

One lifecycle across HubSpot and Salesforce

I owned the field mapping, sync rules, and lifecycle alignment. In June 2025 a custom lead conversion went live: when a lifecycle stage changes in HubSpot, the lead converts in Salesforce automatically. Sales now works from one list, with each lead's stage, score, and marketing activity attached.

  4. OpportunitySales · meeting booked
  5. CustomerClosed won and live

Answers: No shared definition of a sales-ready lead

A lead score built with sales

I drafted fit and engagement criteria, then reviewed each one with the sales team before approval in June 2025. Because sales helped define the score, they trusted it when it appeared in Salesforce.

Answers: No link between activity and revenue

Reporting both teams use

Dashboards in HubSpot, built on synced Salesforce data, gave marketing and sales the same numbers. One view traced the path from first visit to booked meeting, so I could see which articles readers saw before booking. That shaped the content plan and the SEO work on the top 20 pages.

Answers: A leaky demo journey

A demo journey that stays on our site

Booking moved from an outside link to a HubSpot page with the calendar embedded. A routing form sends requests to sales, HR, or patient support. Known details prefill the booking, and each meeting and task lands in Salesforce with its source intact.

Also built. Around the core systems I rebuilt the blog library, the resource and event pages, and the customer stories hub, which moved from WordPress to HubSpot. Site performance work cut page load lag by half.

Told straight

One persona ad campaign produced modest results: 30,000+ ad views and 2 demo requests. I tracked it weekly, ended the weakest segment mid-flight, and moved the budget to the strongest one.
